I am a senior undergraduate in Mechanical Engineering at Seoul National University (SNU). My research interests lie in **soft robotics**, particularly **bio-inspired design, compliant manipulation, and safe physical interaction**, and in **swarm robotics**, where collective behaviors emerge from interactions among many simple bodies. I am also interested in the **materials and structures** that make such robots possible: **architected materials, responsive structures, and predictive modeling**. I am drawn to robots whose capabilities are built into their bodies, where the distribution of stiffness, geometry, and material response shapes how they move and interact with their surroundings.

I am currently a research intern at the **Microfluids & Soft Matter Laboratory** at SNU, advised by Prof. Ho-Young Kim. There, I study how the spatial distribution of joint flexibility governs the locomotion of chain-like robots ("link-bots") through porous media, and how collective behaviors emerge when many link-bots interact. I have also built humidity-responsive shape-morphing textiles and a pore-opening filter using coiled yarn actuators.

Previously, I worked on multi-object robotic grasping at the **SNU Biorobotics Laboratory** with Prof. Kyu-Jin Cho. I contributed to [MOGrip](https://doi.org/10.1126/scirobotics.ado3939) (*Science Robotics*, 2024) and analyzed the dominant modes of human multi-object grasping. As a Summer Undergraduate Research Fellow at **Purdue University**, I developed a joint-weighted A* path planner for a 6-DOF robotic arm as part of a digital-twin framework for safe manufacturing automation, working with Prof. Martin Byung-Guk Jun.

**I am applying to Ph.D. programs for Fall 2027.**
